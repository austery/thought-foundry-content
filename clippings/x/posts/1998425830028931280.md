China builds indigenous silicon regardless of H200. 

The only real variable is whether that progress compounds inside CUDA or outside US control  

and that’s what actually reprices NVDA, HBM names, and the entire AI cycle.

“Treason vs survival” is the wrong frame for H200. People say NVDA is “selling out America” or DC is “gifting China compute.” 

None of that matters. With or without H200, China will build its own silicon. The real trade is…

how much edge the US gives back, and on what terms, to keep control of the game.

If your mental model is “block everything or lose the lead,” you’re still reading the cartoon version. 

You cannot stop indigenous silicon any more than you could stop fabs in Taiwan or Korea. The only question is whether that progress compounds inside your standard or outside your reach.

Cut China off completely and you force a hard break from CUDA. Painful now, but long term you invite a parallel stack that never needs to listen to Washington again.

Ship H200 under caps, tariffs and high prices, and you compress the gap but keep their best engineers and applications living inside CUDA while their money funds your next node.

Underneath that sits the real border: packaging. CoWoS, HBM and binning create a stratified compute world where different blocs get different silicon, different thermals, different latency budgets. US hyperscalers get top shelf; China gets a monetised, constrained tier.

So H200 isn’t about exports. It’s a live test of NVDA’s true moat, process vs ecosystem tax , and how much compute spread policymakers are willing to trade for a controlled landing of the AI cycle.
