## Original text

<pre>The memory sector has been the strongest performer this year. Prices are rising almost monthly and Micron is already up 2x.

So when $MU sells off right after Google drops a new AI model, people assume the worst: “CFO raised Capex. Is this the start of another overbuild?”

That reaction completely misreads where we are in the cycle.

Micron is not dealing with a traditional DRAM boom-bust. It’s sitting inside a multi-year, AI-driven supercycle with demand coming from every direction.
Model sizes are still compounding. 

Inference is just getting started. AI agents and on-device intelligence are about to enter consumer platforms.
Cloud providers, AI chip companies, and even government HPC programs have already locked 3–5 year budgets.
This multi-source, cross-industry demand pattern has never existed in memory history.

The old 2017–2018 playbook doesn’t apply. HBM is not a business you can scale by simply “deciding to expand.”
The stacking process is brutally hard and capacity takes years to qualify.
The industry isn’t expanding recklessly. It’s expanding reluctantly.

Micron’s Capex hike is not aggressive expansion. It’s forced expansion. Customers have already pre-booked most of 2026 supply. HBM is structurally undersupplied and will remain so.

If Micron doesn’t invest now, the real risk isn’t memory oversupply. It’s a bottleneck across the entire AI supply chain: GPUs underutilized, compute nodes delayed, inference costs stuck too high, and model upgrades slowed for everyone.

Capex going up is not a warning sign.
It’s confirmation of overwhelming demand.

We’re nowhere near the point where oversupply is a realistic concern.
The winners of this cycle will be the companies willing to scale within a structurally constrained industry.

This is the real signal from Micron’s latest commentary.</pre>
