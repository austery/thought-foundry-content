## Original text

<pre>Most “value investors” hate charts. They shouldn’t. If you invest on a 5-year horizon, today’s RSI is mostly noise.

But if you ignore technicals completely, you’ll keep overpaying for your own thesis.

My $INTC view is structural, but the short game is execution.

In a fractured world, the U.S. needs a credible domestic manufacturing pole. That’s the long game.

But where can I add exposure with cheap risk, and where am I wrong?

Markets are a coordination game. If we had to meet in NYC with no phones, we’d both end up at Grand Central under the clock. That’s a Schelling point: the default meeting place.

In a market of millions of strangers, nobody can call each other and say: “Buy here.” So the crowd converges on the same obvious landmarks: round numbers, the 50/200DMA, prior highs/lows, and yes, the lazy-looking Fibonacci zones. A Schelling point. Grand Central for liquidity.

$INTC is a clean example of “cheap risk”.

36.3 (Fi 0.618) held as the first defense. 
36.9 (50DMA) is the next tell. 
39.8 (Fi 0.786) is the real wall. Clear it with volume, and 44 becomes a map.

Fundamentals tell you WHAT to own. Technicals tell you WHEN to size up, and where the thesis is invalid.

Conviction is long-term. Execution is tactical. Skip the fancy lines. Use the levels everyone watches.</pre>
