And therein lies the irony of modern life. We are surrounded by comfort, but we feel restless. We are running faster than ever, but we still feel like we are lagging behind. We are connected, but we feel alone. We ask ourselves if everything we do matters. These are four symptoms that I see everywhere I go . Restlessness, feeling left behind, loneliness, invisibility. The real irony of modern life is that a dream and an illness look the same. The first symptom is our feeling of restlessness and anxiety. We all live in this culture of constant rush and exhaustion. No one believes that we need to stop or at least slow down. In 2018, Harvard Business School professor Michael Norton conducted a study involving over 2,000 people. Each of them had a fortune of at least a million dollars. Lucky ones. Many of them had even much more. And he asked each of them two questions. How happy are you on a scale of 1 to 10? And how much more money do you need to raise your happiness level to 10? The second question received the most interesting answer. The person with 1 million said she only needed 3 million to reach 10 happiness points. But the man with 3 million wanted 10 million. And the person with 10 million wanted 30. Regardless of wealth level, everyone wanted about three times as much. So it's never about the number itself. Wherever you are, there will always be a larger number. About a month ago, I was talking to a 24- year-old guy and asked him, "What do you want to be?" His answer was: " Rich." First chase the numbers, and then hope that the meaning will somehow appear later. Our careers, our companies, our relationships—we all do it. So there you have it , the four symptoms : feeling restless, falling behind, lonely, and unnoticed. What is the deep root cause of all this ? There are many reasons, but one of the main ones, in my opinion , is that we tend to replace the real hero with a stand-in. And that's the problem. If you measure the wrong metric, you encourage the wrong behavior. There is a principle called Goodhart's Law. When a metric itself becomes a target for optimization, it ceases to be a good metric. Our modern life is full of such false indicators. Money is a substitute for security and freedom. A position is a substitute for being taken seriously. A company can be a surrogate for creating something that will outlive you. Of course, these stand-ins are not meaningless. Money matters. Cash flow keeps your company alive. This is all good. But some understudy roles become so convincing that we forget the existence of the real hero. So, this is the template. And here's how to break it. The disease called " modern life" requires a four-step treatment. And for that we will have to go back to the 70s. John Bogle founded Vanguard in the 1970s, and it became one of the largest financial institutions in the world. She remains that way. But he gave it a structure almost unheard of in finance. Vanguard will own its funds. And those funds will belong to their investors. It still works that way . He built one of the largest financial firms in history and never owned a stake in the company. He was not confused by doubles and fake indicators. He knew what was real. So how can we do the same? Here are four steps. First, name the symptom. What do you really feel ? Restlessness, lagging behind, loneliness, being unnoticed? This is important because you will often misdiagnose the real problem. For example, when you say you need a raise, do you feel left behind, anxious, unnoticed, or all of the above ? You should know the difference. Step two: Find the need that underlies this symptom. Every symptom has a deep, unspoken need . When you feel anxious, your hidden need may be for safety. When you feel like you're falling behind, your hidden need may be for sufficiency. When you feel lonely, it may be a need for belonging. When you are overlooked, your hidden need may be for significance. So take a walk, examine yourself, write it down. What do you really need? Step three: expose your understudy. What substitute are you pursuing instead of this real need? Because it's easier to measure a substitute. You can count the subscribers, but not the involvement. You can measure how fast you run compared to others, but your own sense of satisfaction is hard to measure . The understudy is attractive precisely because it gives you an indicator. It shows whether the number has increased or decreased, but it cannot tell whether the real need has been met . That's why you need the fourth and final step. Replace your understudy with a real hero. Seek the real directly. If you feel anxious, write down what exactly you expected to gain from this pursuit. When you reach the top of a hill, remember that all you will see is another, bigger hill , and you can continue climbing, but have a clear understanding of why you are doing it. If you feel like you're falling behind, stop comparing yourself to the rest of the world. Compete with yourself. Determine what “good” means to you in the life you actually live . So these are four symptoms, but you see, no matter which one you experience, the action is the same. It's the same four-step process. One clear definition of sufficiency is when you are restless; one race against yourself when you are behind; one person who really knows you; and one life that changed because you were here. If you have these four things, it's unlikely that anything else will matter much. And if they aren't—well, you still have plenty of time.
