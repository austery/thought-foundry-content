Hello everyone , my name is Li Wenli . I am 58 years old , and my pen name is Meng Yu . I come from a small village in Jingning County , Gansu . I wear many hats : a daughter , a daughter-in-law , a mother , a grandmother , a writing enthusiast , and even a self-taught artist who draws whatever comes to mind . In the rural area where I grew up , families were large and poor . Children as young as six or seven had to help out with farm work . When I was a child , I especially loved singing , dancing , and reading picture-story books . I always imagined that when I grew up , I would become a singer or a dancer . But later , I realized that knowing how to cook , sew , endure hardship , and manage a household ... ... was what a rural person was supposed to be like . After graduating from junior high , just like my peers , I stumbled into adulthood , bewildered and unprepared . At the time , my second sister was being beaten half to death by her handsome husband while she was still recovering from childbirth . I thought to myself , my sister had married a stranger she’d known for only a few days . But the man I was to marry was someone I knew ; we had attended the same school , he was a great student , and he loved novels too . I thought , since we shared common interests despite being unfamiliar , surely I wouldn't end up in the same tragic situation as my sister . Later , I realized that the rural men of our generation were deeply chauvinistic . My husband , in particular , was spoiled at home , had a terrible temper , and would resort to violence at the slightest provocation . I remember one time , during an argument , he even hit my ear . Honestly , I thought it was good enough just to be married off . Everyone said I was ugly when I was little , and I felt incredibly inferior and lacked confidence . When we got married , we didn't have wedding photos . We just put two individual portraits together , stamped them , and that was it . Back then , I didn't even have my own name . Because I was " ugly , " people called me names like " Monkey " or " Long-legged Li . " After marriage , they just called me " so-and-so's wife . " In that village , many women didn't have their own names ; they were all just addressed as someone's daughter or someone's wife . Even now , I don't know my own grandmother's name , and I don't know the names of many women in the village . Later , I felt that after getting married , I would end up like my grandmother and mother , trapped in domestic life forever , never seeing the end of it . I gave birth to three children , and before I had even finished my postpartum recovery , my father passed away . Before I could recover from that grief , my husband got into a car accident and broke his leg . Less than a month later , my eldest sister , who was in her fifties , suddenly passed away from a brain hemorrhage . Struck by these repeated blows , I didn't want to say a word ; I felt my fate was exceptionally cruel . Especially since our family's financial situation was poor , I left home for the first time to work , going to many places , taking on odd jobs as a cleaner and working at construction sites . Later , I even ran a small shop , but I didn't earn much money , and I ended up exhausting my own health . At that time , I started wondering if I was just an extra , useless person in this world . Especially after seeing so many of my relatives suddenly disappear from my sight , I wondered : is a person's life destined to be full of suffering until they suddenly vanish ? What is the meaning of being alive anyway ? In the summer of 2017 , I saw an article on my phone . The first sentence , written by a writer named Fan Yusu , said : " My life is a book that is hard to read ; fate has treated me so shabbily . " This sentence hit my lonely , withered heart like a bolt of lightning . I felt this sentence was written for me . I wanted to write too ; I wanted to express the confusion , bewilderment , and struggles in my heart . For the first time , the idea of going to Beijing came to me . Just then , the Women's Federation came to our village to promote opportunities , saying they needed domestic workers and encouraging us women to go out . The chairwoman of the Women's Federation said something to me that stuck : " Going to Beijing won't just help you find work , more importantly , it will broaden your horizons . " So , I made up my mind , used that contact information , and traveled to Beijing all by myself . After arriving in Beijing , I remember very clearly the first time I took the subway . I saw the subway doors beep three times and close ; my bag was almost caught in them , which terrified me . Once inside , there were empty seats nearby , but I was too scared to sit down ; I was extremely nervous . Later , when I learned how to draw , I painted that scene , and I still feel my hands trembling just like then , as if that fear happened only yesterday . Later , when I arrived at the domestic agency , the teacher told us , " Once you're at the employer's , the employer is your master . You should talk less , work more , try not to say unnecessary things , solve your own problems , and don't bother your employer . " Then the teacher asked us to write down our hobbies on paper . I wrote down singing and dancing , because I felt those were indeed my hobbies . The teacher scolded me , saying , " You're here to be a nanny , not to perform . " Frightened , I quickly wrote down " taking care of the elderly and looking after children . " That night , as I slept on the floor at the agency , everyone else slept soundly , but whenever I thought of what the teacher said , I was too scared to fall asleep all night , wondering what I would do once I reached the employer's home . Later , in the group chat set up by the teacher at the agency , I saw many requirements , such as accepting surveillance , obeying arrangements , not talking much , knowing a little English , not being timid , not snoring , not making noise while eating , not being from Northeast China or Henan , having no family members around , trying not to go home , and having one day off per week , or better yet , not taking any breaks . Seeing all this made me feel incredibly panicked . Especially when we go to work , if you are looking after a child and you don't talk or interact with the child , the employer will say , " You’re here to take care of the child , why aren't you saying anything ? " " It's not good for the child . " I also saw some people saying that those looking after children need to be good-looking and even have double eyelids , which I found totally absurd . Later , when I learned how to write , I gathered all these things together and wrote a piece about domestic work . Thank you . Actually , when we sisters chat together , because they are all like me — domestic workers — we feel that when we are in the employer's home , we are not treated as people , but just as labor tools . Also , I once met an employer ; thinking back on it now , it's actually quite funny . Perhaps the male employer was a manager at his workplace , for every night when he returned and I was busy in the kitchen or the bathroom , he would call me over with his wife to hold a meeting with me . And what was the content of these meetings ? He asked me , " How are you getting on in our home today ? How is your mood ? And what are your thoughts ? Tell me about them . " Then , because my Mandarin wasn't standard , they made me download an app to learn it and read a passage out loud in front of them . Actually , I never learned Mandarin as a child ; I only picked it up from my employers after I started working . It felt especially unreliable and even worse when I had to read in front of others . Then he said , " No , you have to master this before you go to sleep . " Later , I felt that cooking , laundry , and childcare in their home were fine — I especially love kids , and the children were close to me — but I couldn't stand the way they treated their dog , so I left after a few days . In reality , we domestic workers are on high alert at every employer's house . Some employers claim their clothes are too expensive for machines and insist we hand-wash them . After a full day of work , our hands and arms ache terribly . Especially after washing clothes , I'd be afraid to touch the baby's face or even the clothes themselves , fearing I might snag them . I drew this once I started painting . One worker told me she was caring for an elderly woman who would complain when she took her Saturday break , saying , " You people from the countryside are used to working at home anyway , right ? You eat well and live well here ; the work isn't tiring , and you're sheltered from the wind and rain . What kind of break do you need ? You're just being too spoiled . " I thought the old lady had a point . I'm from the countryside too — why have I become so delicate since coming to the city ? That’s why I wrote the poem , " Country Woman . " A friend once told me she actually did well at her employer's home . The family had many people with different tastes , and she would diligently prepare meals for them every day . But when it was time to eat , the employer said , " We don't allow the helper to sit at the table with us . " My friend felt so wronged that she told me , " I don't want to do this anymore ; I can't take it . " I’ve been wondering lately , why was I able to stay with my employers for so long ? For one , my employer treats me well , and for another , I think I should be grateful to my chauvinistic husband . One person said she was caring for an elderly person at an employer's home , and just three days after she arrived , the elderly person said , " Oh my , where did the milk go ? Many things I bought are missing . What’s going on here ? " " You people from the countryside really have no manners . " Every employer loves to install cameras . No matter what we do or where we are , that camera is like a ghost , staring at us with that eerie green light . I feel that everything is visible to the employer , so why would they say such things about me ? Later , I felt there was nothing I could do , because they are the masters , so I just painted all of these down . I have also taken care of an old lady who was exceptionally kind . She would read the newspaper to me every day , reading things like the Guangming Daily , the Legal Daily , and the People's Daily . She would say , " Oh my , why is it like this ? " Every day there are reports of nannies mistreating the elderly or the children . Especially when she heard I was from the countryside , from Gansu , she found it very strange . She said , " Many people say Gansu is very poor , so when you came to Beijing from your hometown , how did you get here ? " Actually , I felt very uncomfortable hearing that at the time , but I couldn't refute the elderly lady . If I refuted her , she would get angry , which wouldn't be good for me either . So , to humor her , I just said there was no transportation , and I walked here step by step when I came . Another person told me she had worked for her employer for many years , treated the family's children very well , and the children couldn't do without her . She remembered exactly where everything in the house was kept , and everyone's habits , hobbies , and tastes clearly . But sometimes when leaving , some employers would say , " Open your bag and let me take a look . " Hmm , it just felt really ... how should I say , it was like having a bucket of cold water poured over me from head to toe . I once was at an employer's house ; their place was actually very large and had spare bedrooms , but the employer wouldn't give me one . I asked to stay , but she said the bedroom was reserved for visiting relatives and friends , so she had me sleep on the balcony . The balcony actually just had a curtain pulled across , and a small cot pressed against the window . One night in winter , it was incredibly cold , the wind was howling , and I couldn't sleep . So , I wrapped myself in my quilt and sat on the iron bed , looking out at the brilliantly lit street , with people coming and going , and so many cars . I was thinking , among all those people , no one would ever imagine that in this tiny room , there’s a rural woman in her 50s , shivering and unable to sleep , just sitting here wrapped in a quilt . Later , when I painted this picture , that freezing night felt as if it were still chilling my body . Actually , from the day I left that house , none of their relatives or friends had even been there . Once , I worked for an employer who was very kind ; they had a small utility room they cleared out for me to stay in . That room had no windows , and in the summer , it was exceptionally stifling and hot . One night , after I finished a day’s work and lay down on the bed , I felt suffocated and terribly uncomfortable . A second ago , I was lamenting how bitter my life was , suffering and toiling my whole life , but the next second , I was suddenly inspired , feeling that this wasn't suffering at all , but a form of freedom , because this night belonged to me . Then I typed a poem on my phone , saying , " Oh , this is wonderful ! " My back doesn't ache , my legs don't hurt , my ears don't ring , I don't have to cook , I don't have to do laundry , and I’m not bothered by any tasks . I can soar through the sky or dive into the earth , dance on the clouds , sing on the branches , and swim in the water with the fish . Later , I painted a picture based on this poem . In reality , when we are at our employers ' homes , we have to tie our hair up tightly every day , afraid of hair falling into the food or getting stuck on the children's toys , and we always wear loose , baggy clothes for convenience . But in that moment , I painted myself and my sisters with our hair flying free , some strands even brushing against our faces , and not even wearing clothes , reflecting how we yearn for that kind of relaxation , freedom , and being unburdened , comfortable , and at ease . I. We spend every day in the employers ' living rooms , bedrooms , kitchens , and bathrooms , endlessly repeating the work of washing , cleaning , and scrubbing ; truthfully , most of the time , our hearts feel exceptionally depressed . But then one day , after I finished cleaning my employer's windows and floors , I suddenly realized : am I really just cleaning windows and floors ? I was clearly cleaning an entire building , cleaning my way into the bright , clear sky . In that tiny moment , my heart suddenly opened up . I felt that our work was exceptionally meaningful and truly valuable . Later , I started drawing all of these things down . Actually , I had never painted before . I just remember when I was a child helping my mother with the stove , I would draw in the ashes with a piece of firewood , or draw in the snow , but I had never properly drawn on paper like this . It was only later that an artist teacher saw my drawings and gave me colored pencils and paper to work with , and that's when I started drawing more and more . Looking at the sisters around me , even though they work as housekeepers , in my eyes , they are like water , like trees , and like high-strength cement . They can walk into any kitchen , put on an apron , and immediately whip up a feast ; I think they are truly amazing . The reason I’ve drawn so many pictures and written so many poems is that in 2018 , I joined a worker literature group called the Picun Literature Group , and I’ve been there ever since . At the time , I just wanted to learn how to write to express the things inside my heart . In fact , when we sisters get together and chat , the things we hear are often quite painful . For instance , one said she injured her foot while cleaning windows at her employer's home , but the employer immediately called and had her replaced by someone else . Another said that when she held the baby , she would sometimes sit on the lady's bed , but she noticed that whenever she got up , the lady would pull off the sheets and make her wash them , which made her feel very hurt . Whenever I hear the sisters share these stories , my heart feels very heavy as well . So , later on , I wrote the first poem of my life , titled " We Are a Group of Housekeepers . " Later , because of my feelings , I lost another employer ; the lady told me , " When you cook in the morning , be quieter ; don't disturb our rest . " And then every morning when I get up , I lift my feet up high and walk as softly as a thief . One day , when I drew this , I wondered if my employer would see me like a thief in this monitor and laugh out loud . So I wrote another poem called " Walking Like a Thief . " Actually , I used to be a very timid and insecure person , perhaps because my parents often fought when I was little . I remember after school , whenever I reached our doorstep and heard loud noises from inside , I was too scared to go in , which made me very insecure . I didn't even dare to say a single word in front of others . But later , as I wrote and read more , I felt I had more confidence and courage . When looking for work afterward , I dared to speak up to employers , telling them , " I must rest on Saturdays to attend my classes , and you have to pay me the right wages . " I feel like I have become more and more confident and have changed myself . Now , I like painting even more . This painting was done once when I rented a room near our literature group to make attending classes easier ; it was the first time I had my own free space . I remembered what Virginia Woolf said : for a woman to write fiction , she needs two things : money and a room of her own . At that time , although I didn't have much money , I had my own room and my own free space , so I painted many pictures in that rented place . Because when I was a child , I saw many women in movies wearing beautiful dresses , I felt that when I grew up and had money , I would buy myself a beautiful dress , so in my paintings , I always depict myself and my sisters in the brightest clothes and prettiest dresses , making them look very beautiful . Thank you . Once I wondered , why do we rural women work hard all our lives , and why is our treatment not as good as that of women in the city ? And so I thought , I also want to be myself , I want to be bolder , and I want to bloom . To bloom in my own way , I should be brave enough to be myself and not be afraid of anything , which is why I painted these two pictures . In 2024 , I returned to my hometown . Actually , at that time , I was feeling very conflicted inside . Back home , people were very kind and warm to me . But whenever I thought about leaving Beijing , leaving my teachers and classmates from the literature group , and leaving the friends I could talk to , I still felt a deep sense of soul-crushing loneliness . On the other hand , when I was taking care of my employer's child , I felt that I raised them so well and so attentively . Especially since my own child was so young and I had never taken good care of them , I felt very guilty . At that moment , I wondered if I was a very fragmented person . It was like when I was in the kitchen , there was a broken mirror , and the reflection of me inside looked just like that , so I painted this picture . Later I thought , maybe most people also feel very fragmented inside at some point . In 2024 , I injured my foot . When I finally went to see a doctor , they said I had missed the window for treatment . I think this might be the influence of the older generation I grew up with , especially my grandmother , my mother , and my mother-in-law . No matter how sick they were , as long as they could still speak , they would keep cooking , keep working , and keep enduring it . So I felt that I must value my own body in the future . From then on , I started to paint the pain in my own body . In our rural village back in my day , women had to obey men . Some men would hit women at the drop of a hat , and many people would say , " He hits you because you are disobedient , because you don't take him seriously . " Actually , I only learned later that this might be called domestic violence . However , I had painted a few of those scenes , but later I didn't want to anymore . I remembered what my mother and others often said . They would say , " That's just how life is for women . We've lived our whole lives this way ; just endure it and it will pass . Even teeth and tongues , as close as they are , still bite each other sometimes . " So , after that , I never painted scenes like that again . Actually , there is one kind of pain I will never be able to forget . When I was giving birth to my second child , it happened to be on my birthday . By evening — I had been working in the fields the day before — my stomach started hurting , then the power suddenly went out , and it was windy and snowing . After I delivered the baby , the placenta wouldn't come out , and I didn't know what to do , watching the blood pool before me . I told my mother-in-law , " Take the quilt away . " Because we were really poor in the countryside then , and it wasn't easy to get a new quilt , I didn't want to ruin it . But it was bitterly cold , and that is why I painted this picture . Later , when my husband finally brought the doctor back , I had already caught a cold from the chill . I think this image is one that I will always ... Actually , many women in our village endure hardship and suffering their whole lives , never once living for themselves . I used to work in the fields often , and later , when I was at home , I was always busy carrying my children . Doing laundry , cooking , and everything else ; eventually , my arms ached , my waist ached , my legs ached , my feet ached — there wasn't a part of me that didn't hurt . But during the day , when I was busy working , it didn't seem as bad , but it would become unbearable at night . Later I thought , I couldn't just keep talking about this pain , so I decided to express it through my art , using colors to pour out and paint all of this pain away . When I am in a bad mood , I also paint some dark , gray-toned works . One night , when it was pouring rain , my feet ached and my arms ached so much I couldn't sleep . It was already two in the morning , so I got up and paced back and forth , then painted this picture , feeling like I was hanging off a cliff , unable to go up or come down , so I felt I just had to paint it . To put it this way , I feel that women can never truly break free from their own constraints . Take my mother , for instance , she’s 90. She broke her leg the year before last , so we put her in a nursing home and hired a caregiver to stay with her 24/7 . Yet , Mom often wakes up in the middle of the night , panicked , saying , " Oh no , why am I lying here ? I haven’t cooked , I haven’t fed the chickens , I haven't checked on my vegetable garden — how can I be here ? " Hearing that really touches me . I feel that in her life , what has always mattered most wasn't her own well-being , but the work she felt she had to do . In reality , I have already said goodbye to my past self ; I have already broken free . When I say " broken free , " I don't mean leaving home or going out into the world ; I mean that my mind has emerged from its former narrow perspective . Yet in my dreams , I still see scenes where I haven't finished my chores or haven't done things right , and people even blame me for it , which wakes me up with a start . In 2024 , I actually published the first book of my life while working in Beijing . At the time , it felt like a dream ; I never imagined that what I wrote could ever turn into printed pages . When I brought the book home to show my mom , she didn't believe it at first , but then I told her , " I really wrote this , and I even wrote about your story . " Then , Mom couldn't stop beaming with joy . To everyone she saw , she’d say , " Look , my daughter wrote this , my daughter has become a writer ! " How could a mother not be happy ? Her daughter — the one people looked down on , the one who suffered and struggled — had finally earned some respect for herself . She certainly ... Honestly , I never expected that literature would change me , allowing a simple country person like me to come this far . Actually , I sometimes feel a sense of emptiness , thinking that this life doesn't feel like my own . Furthermore , the further I go and the more people I meet , the more I realize how much I still lack in terms of knowledge and writing skills . But I tell myself , I must not turn back ; I must keep moving forward . Later , I realized that people , whether from the countryside or anywhere else , can all look toward a bigger world . Actually , I remember when my two sons were 30 and still unmarried , many people would scold and blame me , feeling that as a mother , I was being extremely irresponsible . At the time , I wondered , why should a mother have to bear this responsibility ? Especially since I had heard since childhood that " women should be this way , women should be that way ; this is what women are for , that is what women are for . " I felt really unconvinced by that . Once , in a fit of impulse , I wrote a poem : " If I were given a stage , although I am not well-educated , I would share my life from birth until now , my one-of-a-kind , unrestrained journey , and give a speech . " But , I never expected that today I would finally be standing on this stage . Of course , I still feel like I am dreaming . But at this moment , I am truly standing on this stage , and having my sons in the audience listening to my life story makes me feel truly happy . Thank you everyone .
